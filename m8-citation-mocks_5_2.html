Citation in Slack

A design exploration of how m8 shows where an answer comes from — so a person in Slack can trust it, check it, and act on it without leaving the conversation. Nothing here is final: it needs a read from product and engineering before it goes to build.

  • What Slack gives us — the native citation chip, how it renders, and what m8 can and can't change.
  • How to cite — the marker styles, and how to handle answers that rest on many sources.
  • How it behaves — while the answer streams, on hover, and when someone opens the source itself.

Every mock is built from Slack's own message anatomy, so what's shown can ship. They're live — hover a chip, click a source, expand a list. Feedback welcome on any of it.

How Slack renders a citation natively

Slack's native citation element renders as one grey chip with three fixed parts. m8 supplies the data; Slack draws the chip and owns its behavior.

1Absence balance
1 · Number

The index — the citation's position in the answer. It ties the inline chip to the same row in the Sources footer, and shows how many sources back the answer.

Always shown · m8 sets the value
2 · Icon

Picked by Slack from the source type. For an outside system like Workday, m8 passes app_icon_url and Slack shows that logo; for Slack messages, files and web pages Slack uses its own glyph.

Slack-chosen · logo only via app_icon_url
3 · Label

The text field — a short source name. Keep it to two or three words; it is what makes the chip wide.

m8 sets the text
  • Hover (desktop) or tap (mobile) opens Slack's preview card: title, source, snippet, open link.
  • Click opens the record, message or file. Every chip is interactive — there is no static version.
  • Where it can sit: in a sentence, in a numbered Sources list, or at the end of a quoted excerpt (rich_text only).
  • When it appears: on the final message only — citations attach at chat.stopStream, not while text is streaming.
  • Not adjustable: shape, colour, size, or a custom icon. The chip looks the same as Slack AI's own citations.
  • If the native element isn't available: fall back to plain text markers — ¹ or [1] as links — with no preview.

Citation options

The native chip's look is Slack's to control. Options 1 and 2 are the two text fallbacks Block Kit supports when the native element isn't available — both are plain rich_text links. Hover any marker to see the preview card (tap on mobile); click opens the source in a new tab. Shown on all four so they compare like-for-like; note that only the native chip actually gets that card in Slack. Pill, circle-badge and icon chips were dropped: no inline styling exists for them. Options 3 and 4 are the preferred directions — the native chip with a label, and the same chip reduced to number and icon; 1 and 2 stay as fallbacks.

Option 1 — superscript

Buildable: Unicode superscript digits (¹ ² ³) as rich_text link text. No hover preview; single digits only.

Buildable: "[1]" as a rich_text link — renders in Slack link blue and opens the source. No hover preview; works in plain mrkdwn too.

Multiple citations

Two ways to present an answer that draws on several sources. Both keep one chip per claim in the text; they differ in how the sources are listed underneath. Option 1 is the preferred direction. Every chip here is live: hover for the preview, click to open.

Option 1 — multiple chipsPreferred

Each claim keeps its own inline chip, and a Sources footer lists all of them in citation order — each chip already carries its source icon, so no grouping is needed. The footer is sent in full; when the message runs long, Slack itself truncates it and adds its native Show more link — no button, no round-trip to m8. Slack chooses where the fold lands; here it's shown after the first row of chips.

While streaming

Slack streams plain text only; blocks — and native citation chips — can be attached only when the stream closes. So the question is what the reader sees before the chips arrive.

While streaming, citations are [n] markdown links — blue and clickable. On finalize the same text is re-sent with native chips in the same positions. Visible from the first sentence; a small reflow at the end.

Recommendation

Option 3: the AI steps timeline runs ahead of the text — "Searching Workday…" with the found sources listed as links — then the answer streams and chips and footer land on finalize. Assign the indices when retrieval completes so numbers never change between states.

Why. The reader sees what was searched and found before the answer arrives, the sources are visible even while the text has no chips, and the final swap reads as an upgrade rather than a rewrite.

Rule of thumb

Excerpt under the message as the default. The reader modal when a passage needs its surrounding section. The sources canvas as the audit record for multi-source answers. Workday as the destination for acting, not reading.

All of them need m8 to store the exact cited passage with the citation index, so the passage is verbatim, never regenerated.

Domestic relocation program

Updated Jun 3, 2026 · People Ops · Applies to US employees

3.1 Eligibility

Full-time employees relocating 50 miles or more at the company's request, or with manager approval for a role change, are eligible for relocation support.

3.2 Stipend

Employees approved for a domestic relocation receive a one-time stipend of $10,000, paid with the first payroll at the new work location. The stipend is taxable income and does not require receipts. Amounts are reviewed annually and may differ for international moves.

3.3 Timing

Requests must be approved in Workday before the move date. Stipends approved after the move are paid on the next regular payroll.

Rules of thumb

  • Dedupe by source, not by retrieved chunk; cap at 3–5 cited sources per answer.
  • One chip per claim, at the end of the sentence or clause — never a cluster.
  • Footer lists every source in citation order — no group headers, the chip icon says where it's from; long footers fold under Slack's native "Show more".
  • Citations attach on chat.stopStream only — streamed text has none, chips appear when the answer finalizes.
  • Never let the model produce URLs; inject verified source metadata after generation and validate every index maps to a real source.
